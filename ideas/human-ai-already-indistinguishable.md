# We already can't tell humans from AI

## Current understanding
Asked whether one day we won't be able to tell humans from AI, their answer
is that we already can't. They don't know "at what level" the question is
meant. Their example: in autumn 2026 a French literary prize went to an
author who hid having used AI.

## Research (external, not personal perspective)
Checked 2026-10-23 by web search.
- Thélyson Orélien, *C'était ça ou mourir* (Grasset), won the Prix du roman Fnac 2026. After an alert from the X account "Balance ton Claude", the Pangram detector judged it written almost entirely by AI; the Académie Goncourt removed it from its selection. His Canadian publisher confirmed AI was used and parted ways with him. The author still denies it. Sources: [Hebdo39](https://hebdo39.net/rubrique-grands-mots-grands-remedes-une-sortie-de-route-au-grand-prix/), [AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/prix-pascal-nouveau-prix-litt%C3%A9raire-095519000.html), [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/culture/livres-bd/2026/10/21/le-prix-pascal-recompensera-uniquement-des-oeuvres-ecrites-avec-lia-HUID7I47VJDXLCKY7FTA2FVVNA/).
- The Fnac prize is the one that crowned it; "critics" in their drop is a chat shorthand. The point (a jury did not see it) stands.
- Aftermath: a new "Prix Pascal" for fiction co-written with AI, with declared use, entries from Nov 2, awarded early December ([Journal du Geek](https://www.journaldugeek.com/2026/10/22/apres-le-scandale-goncourt-ce-prix-veut-recompenser-le-meilleur-roman-ia/)).
- Irony worth noting, not theirs: humans couldn't tell, a detector (an AI) did.
