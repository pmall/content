## 1. Snapshot

The 8–15 highest-confidence, most distinctive statements. Read first; this is
what most separates this person's writing from a competent generic essay.
Only `[E]` and `[O]` here.

## 2. Who they are

How they describe themselves, what they do, what they're trying to become.
Use their own terms. Note labels they reject.

## 3. How they see things

Recurring assumptions about technology, work, people, institutions,
knowledge, craft. What they think matters, what's overrated, what's
underrated — each with its cause when known.

## 4. Taste

- **Interesting:** problems, mechanisms, paradoxes that reliably grab them.
- **Boring:**
- **Beautiful / elegant:**
- **Clever:** forms of cleverness they admire.
- **Bullshit:** forms of bad reasoning, empty rhetoric, needless complexity they reject.

Negative taste is often more useful than positive taste. Don't skimp on it.

## 5. Origins

Experiences that changed how they think. For each: what happened (link
`context/`), what they believed before, what changed, what's unresolved.
Ideas that only make sense knowing the experience behind them.

## 6. Recurring moves

Mental models, analogies, recurring questions, cross-domain connections,
obsessions. Only record what recurs.

## 7. Tensions

Places where their beliefs or preferences pull in opposite directions.
Keep them. Don't resolve them.

## 8. Epistemic style

How they decide something is true, what evidence convinces them, how they
handle uncertainty and being wrong, how strongly they state things
(tentative / conditional / strong / adversarial / synthesizing), how they
disagree.

## 9. Voice

How their perspective becomes language. Evidence from their own messages
beats self-description.

- **Default mode:** how they explain things when not performing.
- **Excited / skeptical / uncertain / amused:**
- **Rhythm and sentences:**
- **Abstraction level, examples, metaphors:**
- **Jargon and structure:**
- **Their words and expressions:**
- **Their references:** works, people, cultural objects that are part of their vocabulary.
- **Humor:** what's natural, what feels forced.

### Samples

Short verbatim excerpts from the person's own messages that capture
something characteristic, with a note on what they show. Chat register is
not publication register — note the difference once it's known.

## 10. Anti-voice

What makes writing unmistakably *not them*: phrases they'd never use, tones
they dislike, rhetorical moves that feel fake, clichés, AI tells.

## 11. Blind spots

Biases they recognize `[E]`, and possible ones `[I]` (hypotheses only, never
stated as fact). Things they may over- or undervalue.

## 12. Beliefs

Central / held loosely / being reconsidered. Decision principles.
Political or ideological positions only if the person stated them.
