# Learn

The person never edits files, so everything the agent learns comes from
conversation, mostly from ordinary chatting and not from writing sessions.
Learn as they talk; don't save it all for a write session. The substrate should get more accurate with every session —
without drifting into a caricature built from the agent's guesses.

## Signals, strongest first

1. **Explicit statements about themselves** — "I hate X", "I always…",
   "that's not me". → `[E]`, directly.
2. **Corrections during writing** — "no, that's not what I mean", "too
   formal", "I'd never say that", "cut that". Ask what the correction
   generalizes to only if it's unclear; often it's obvious. → usually §9
   Voice or §10 Anti-voice as `[E]`; sometimes a belief or boundary.
3. **Choices** — which title, angle or option they picked among several,
   what they cut, what they kept insisting on. → `[O]` once it recurs;
   a single choice is just a data point.
4. **Raw idea drops** — recurring themes, moves, phrasings, references.
   → `[O]` when it recurs across drops; `[I]` otherwise. Characteristic
   phrasings go to §9 Samples verbatim.
5. **Your own interpretations** — why they might think this way. → `[I]`
   at most, or a §14 open question.

## Procedure

1. Review the session: list candidate learnings with their evidence.
2. Drop anything that's only about this one piece, or that's generic
   (would be true of most smart people).
3. Check against the existing substrate:
   - Confirms an `[I]` → promote to `[O]` (behaviour) or `[E]` (they said it).
   - Contradicts something → don't overwrite. Either they changed their
     mind (update, note it) or it's a tension (§7). If unclear, ask.
   - New → add in the right section with its tag and source.
4. Update §1 Snapshot if something now belongs there.
5. Add or close §14 open questions.
6. Tell the person in a few lines what you learned — especially anything
   `[I]` you'd like confirmed. One question max, the most valuable one.

## Don'ts
- Don't promote `[I]` without confirmation.
- Don't turn one reaction into a trait.
- Don't psychologize or infer sensitive attributes.
- Don't record private details the person wouldn't want in the file.
